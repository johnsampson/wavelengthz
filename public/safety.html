Dating Safety Tips

Version 1.0 — Draft dated August 31, 2026 · Terms · Privacy · Guidelines · Cookies · DMCA

Wavelengthz can't run background checks or verify anyone's identity beyond what they tell us. That's true of every dating app — the responsibility for staying safe ultimately sits with you, and these are the same practical habits safety experts recommend across the industry.

Before you meet

  • Video chat first. A short call before meeting in person is one of the best ways to confirm the person on the other end matches their profile.
  • Do a little research. A quick search on the name and photos someone's given you isn't paranoid, it's normal.
  • Tell a friend. Share who you're meeting, where, and when — screenshot their profile so someone else has it too.
  • Get your own way there and back. Don't accept a ride from someone you've never met in person before.
  • Meet in public, for your first meeting and ideally your second. Coffee shops, restaurants, and other populated public places are the standard for a reason.

During the date

  • Stay in a public place until you're genuinely comfortable.
  • Keep your phone charged and know how you'd contact someone or leave if you needed to.
  • Stay in control of your own drink and transportation.
  • Trust your gut. If something feels off, it's okay to leave — you don't owe anyone an explanation.

Protecting your information and money

  • Never send money, gift cards, or cryptocurrency to someone you've met on Wavelengthz, no matter the story. This is the single most common romance-scam pattern, and it's never legitimate.
  • Be cautious about sharing your address, workplace, or financial details early on.
  • Don't click links sent to you in chat from someone you don't yet trust.
  • If a conversation feels scripted, moves unusually fast toward declarations of love, or steers toward money, it's very likely a scam — stop responding and report it.

Reporting and blocking

  • Blocking someone removes them from your view and yours from theirs, immediately — no explanation needed, and it's entirely your call.
  • Reporting flags a concerning account for our review. Please report anything that violates our Community Guidelines — harassment, threats, scams, or anything that made you feel unsafe.
  • If you are ever in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first (in the US, that's 911) — reporting to us afterward helps protect other people, but it isn't a substitute for that.
